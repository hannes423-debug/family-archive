Why living people are not shown

This site is public. The people in it are not all dead, and the ones who are alive did not ask to be on the internet.

So the published tree shows living people by surname only. Their given names, dates of birth, birth places, occupations and notes are stripped out before anything is committed. You can see that they exist and where they sit in the family — that is what makes the tree legible — but nothing that identifies them individually is here.

How "living" is decided

  • A recorded death or burial means the person is treated as deceased and is shown in full.
  • Otherwise, someone born more than 100 years ago is presumed deceased — the same threshold Ancestry and MyHeritage use.
  • Everyone else is treated as living, including anyone with no dates recorded at all. When the data is ambiguous the rule errs toward withholding, because the other kind of mistake publishes a real person's details.

What that means in practice

The redaction happens in the generator, not in the browser. The published tree.json never contains the withheld values, so they cannot be recovered by reading the page source, disabling JavaScript, or fetching the data file directly. There is no hidden field and no client-side filter to switch off.

A deploy is also gated on an independent check that re-reads the generated file and fails the build if a living person still carries a name, a date, a place or a note. Nothing reaches this site if that check does not pass.

The source data

The underlying GEDCOM — the full, unredacted family record — is not in the public repository and never has been. It lives offline. What is published here is a derived, deliberately reduced view of it.

If you are in this tree

If you would rather not appear at all, even as a surname, that is a reasonable thing to ask and it will be honoured. Ask the family member who maintains this archive and the record will be removed from the published view.
